# Ten Ox-Herding Pictures framework — canonical content (NUM-301)

The reference the Ox-Herding Map and `/ox-herding-questionnaire` draw from. **Use as reference, not script.** The map output should match where the user actually is, in their language. Never quote these layers verbatim.

Frame for audiences drawn to Zen, Chan, Buddhist meditation, and contemplative practice generally. The pictures work as pictures: no prior knowledge of the tradition is needed to recognise oneself in one. Keep stage names and classical terms out of question copy; they appear on result screens and in maps, translated names primary, Chinese (and Japanese reading) secondary.

**Core principle — where you are, not who you are.** This map reads a *current position* among ten pictures, not a fixed identity. Searching is not "behind." Working is not "less than." Each picture is the whole path, seen from where the person stands. The sequence is not a ranking, and people move back through it as often as forward. A person can stand in different pictures in different parts of their life.

**The map ends in the marketplace, not in emptiness.** Kakuan deliberately added two pictures after the empty circle (picture 8) to correct the misreading of emptiness as the destination. Suchness (9) and the return to ordinary life with open hands (10) are not a step down from emptiness. They are what emptiness is for. Never call 8 or 9 "enlightenment" and 10 "afterwards."

**The empty circle is never claimed from a single glimpse.** A vivid moment of the self dropping away is a glimpse (picture 3 territory, or a Familiar touch of the Empty band), not a home. Place at 8 or 9 only on repeated, lived, everyday evidence, never on one striking entry or one dramatic sitting.

**The ox is never actually separate.** The ox stands for true nature, the mind, what is being sought. It only seemed to be lost. Hold the image lightly and do not reify it.

## The path

Ten pictures attributed to the Chan master Kakuan Shien (廓庵師遠), twelfth-century China, illustrated in many woodblock series since. Grouped into **five bands** for the questionnaires (the Q1 tally and Q2/Q3 state-per-band work on bands; the tiebreakers and the Q3 image question resolve the specific picture).

| # | Band | Picture | Chinese | Japanese reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Searching | Searching for the Ox | 尋牛 | Jingyu |
| 2 | Searching | Seeing the Tracks | 見跡 | Kenseki |
| 3 | Working | Catching Sight of the Ox | 見牛 | Kengyū |
| 4 | Working | Catching the Ox | 得牛 | Tokugyū |
| 5 | Working | Taming the Ox | 牧牛 | Bokugyū |
| 6 | Riding | Riding the Ox Home | 騎牛帰家 | Kigyū Kika |
| 7 | Riding | The Ox Transcended | 忘牛存人 | Bōgyū Sonnin |

| 9 | Empty | Returning to the Source | 返本還源 | Henpon Gengen |
| 10 | Marketplace | Entering the Marketplace | 入鄽垂手 | Nitten Suishu |

Band themes (used in Q2/Q3 per-band readings):

| Band | Theme |
|---|---|
| Searching | longing, tracks, the first turn inward |
| Working | glimpse, struggle, and steady taming |
| Riding | ease, harmony, and the ox fading from view |
| Empty | both forgotten, returning to source |
| Marketplace | ordinary life with open hands |

Default picture per band (used when a band is placed but no tiebreaker or resolution question settled the picture): Searching → 1, Working → 3, Riding → 6, Empty → 8, Marketplace → 10.

Visual motif for the map: a horizontal arc of ten pictures, read left to right, with the empty circle (○) at 8 and the arc bending back down to ground level at 10. The marketplace sits at the same height as the start, on purpose.

## 1. Searching for the Ox — 尋牛 *Jingyu*

- **Meaning:** The restless sense that something essential is missing, and the choice to go looking for it. That someone is here, mapping their inner life, is this picture in action.
- **The image:** A figure alone in tall grass, pushing it aside, looking for something they have never seen. Rivers swelling, mountains far off, no idea which way to go.
- **Lived markers:** A restless feeling that something essential is missing; turning inward, beginning to question; hunger for a teaching or a direction without yet having one.
- **Gift:** Sincerity. The longing itself is the first movement of the path. The ox was never truly lost, only the seeker's awareness of it.
- **Pitfall:** Chasing teachings not yet needed; mistaking the next book or retreat for the thing itself.

## 2. Seeing the Tracks — 見跡 *Kenseki*

- **Meaning:** Clues start turning up, in books, teachers, practices. The truth isn't held yet, but it's clearly real and there's a trail.
- **The image:** The same figure crouched over hoofprints in mud. Evidence, at last, that the thing is real.
- **Lived markers:** Meeting teachings, a teacher, or a practice; intellectual understanding beginning to dawn; recognising that the tracks point inward and are everywhere, while the ox itself stays unseen.
- **Gift:** Orientation. Language and direction for what was only a restless ache.
- **Pitfall:** Mistaking understanding for seeing. Reading about the ox becomes a substitute for looking.

## 3. Catching Sight of the Ox — 見牛 *Kengyū*

- **Meaning:** A first direct glimpse of one's true nature. Brief, maybe fleeting, but unmistakable. Proof that what's being sought is already here.
- **The image:** A glimpse of hindquarters through trees. Not the whole animal. Enough to know. A bird singing on a branch, warm sun, green willows.
- **Lived markers:** A moment of direct experience beyond concept, in meditation or in ordinary life (a sound, a flower seen as itself); the glimpse fades, but its imprint stays.
- **Gift:** Certainty that it is real, from direct experience rather than report.
- **Pitfall:** Treating the glimpse as arrival, or chasing the repeat of the experience instead of doing the work it asks for.

## 4. Catching the Ox — 得牛 *Tokugyū*

- **Meaning:** The hard part. Meeting wild, resistant habits face to face. Insight alone isn't enough now; it takes sustained, sometimes exhausting effort.
- **The image:** Both hands on a rope, heels dug in, an animal that has no intention of stopping.
- **Lived markers:** Sustained effort and discipline in practice; wrestling with deep-seated habits, attachments, and resistances; the mind rebelling against its own freedom.
- **Gift:** Contact. You're not lost anymore. You're working.
- **Pitfall:** Forcing, mistaking effort for progress. The ox cannot be dragged, only walked alongside.

## 5. Taming the Ox — 牧牛 *Bokugyū*

- **Meaning:** The struggle softens into steady discipline. Old patterns pull less, and gentle, consistent practice begins to replace force.
- **The image:** Walking beside the animal with the rope slack, still holding it, no longer pulling.
- **Lived markers:** Practice deepening; awareness becoming more continuous; compassion and clarity beginning to guide daily life; the ox following more willingly.
- **Gift:** Workability. The mind is becoming trustworthy.
- **Pitfall:** Letting go of the rope too early, or gripping it long after it's needed. Patience is the work.

## 6. Riding the Ox Home — 騎牛帰家 *Kigyū Kika*

- **Meaning:** Effort turns to ease. Increasingly at home in one's own nature; practice feels less like work and more like riding gently homeward.
- **The image:** Riding home in the evening playing a flute, not steering, the animal knowing the way.
- **Lived markers:** Practice that feels natural and joyful; no longer grasping for awakening or pushing away delusion; a deep contentment in ordinary activities.
- **Gift:** Trust. Harmony between mind and life.
- **Pitfall:** Inflation, mistaking ease for arrival; or coasting. Checking whether the ease is still there is its own kind of grip.

## 7. The Ox Transcended — 忘牛存人 *Bōgyū Sonnin*

(Literally "ox forgotten, person remains.")

- **Meaning:** The methods fall away. There's nothing left to tame, so the seeking quiets and one simply rests as oneself. The ox forgotten, the person remains.
- **The image:** The figure alone at home. The animal is nowhere in the picture and is not missed. Whip and rope lie idle.
- **Lived markers:** The distinction between practice and non-practice dissolving; no more reference to awakening as something to attain; a simple, serene ordinariness.
- **Gift:** Rest. The tool is no longer needed.
- **Pitfall:** A subtle self still sitting there, quietly pleased with its serenity.

## 8. Both Ox and Self Transcended — 人牛倶忘 *Ningyū Gubō*

(Literally "person and ox both forgotten." The empty circle, ensō.)

- **Meaning:** Even the sense of a separate "you" doing the work dissolves into openness, beyond self and other, nothing to grasp or defend.
- **The image:** An empty circle on a blank page.
- **Lived markers:** Seeker and sought both dropped away; no trace of self and no trace of awakening; spaciousness that holds everything.
- **Gift:** Freedom from grasping. The spaciousness itself.
- **Pitfall:** Mistaking this for the destination. Kakuan warned against it, which is why two pictures follow. Also: using emptiness to skip the world, detachment dressed as realisation. The people around the person often notice it as distance.

## 9. Returning to the Source — 返本還源 *Henpon Gengen*

- **Meaning:** Things are simply as they are again. Mountains are mountains, rivers are rivers, but seen clearly now, without the old distortions.
- **The image:** A branch in blossom by running water. No figure at all. The world simply as it is.
- **Lived markers:** The ordinary world perceived freshly; everything sacred precisely because nothing is special; water flows downhill, the sun rises.
- **Gift:** Suchness. The world returns vivid and plain.
- **Pitfall:** Resting in the beauty of suchness as a private view, and not taking the last step back among people.

## 10. Entering the Marketplace — 入鄽垂手 *Nitten Suishu*

(Literally "entering the town with helping hands.")

- **Meaning:** Return to ordinary life with open hands, helping others naturally. Not withdrawal from the world but full-hearted presence within it.
- **The image:** An unremarkable person walking into a market with open hands, indistinguishable from anyone else. Barefoot, dusty, smiling, among fishmongers and innkeepers.
- **Lived markers:** Living fully in the ordinary world with warmth and humour; no outward appearance of holiness; useful to others without performing it.
- **Gift:** Usefulness without specialness. Life as practice.
- **Pitfall:** The pride of having returned, secretly enjoying "having come back." Authentic picture 10 does not announce itself. A person here is not "highest." They are back among the rest of us.

## Transitions and their practices (Q3 "the edge you're at", and the map's "edge")

One crossing per band. Use the matching practice when naming the edge; adapt the wording to the user, keep the substance.

| Crossing | From band | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| From finding tracks to taking hold | Searching | Stop reading about it. Pick one practice you already know how to do and do it daily for a month, badly. Understanding is not what is missing. |
| From gripping the rope to walking beside | Working | Find the place in your practice where you are still forcing, and do it with two-thirds of the effort for a week. Notice what actually falls apart, which is usually less than you fear. |
| From riding home to the ox no longer being there | Riding | Stop taking the temperature of your own progress. The checking is the last piece of the rope, and it is the one that keeps the animal in the picture. |
| From the empty circle back into the world | Empty | Do something small and thoroughly unspiritual for someone who cannot thank you, and do not tell anyone. Kakuan put two pictures after the circle for a reason. |
| From returning to being genuinely unremarkable | Marketplace | Notice the moment you want to be recognised as the one who has come back. Not to suppress it, just to see how often it turns up. |

## Placement (from evidence)

- Place the user at one **current picture**, with a soft halo on the adjacent picture(s) in the same band, and an **edge** (the next crossing) where there's signal.
- Be conservative at the far end of the arc. Pictures 7, 8, and 9 are the easiest to claim from reading and the hardest to live. Words like "void," "empty," or "oneness" in an entry are not evidence of picture 8; a single dramatic sitting is a glimpse. Look for repeated, lived, everyday evidence across time.
- Be equally careful with picture 10. Service language alone is not the marketplace. The signal is ordinariness without specialness, which tends to show up as an absence of spiritual self-reference rather than an abundance of it.
- Two equally lit pictures in different domains of life is a real reading, not an error. Say so.

## Reading diagnostics — the two ends of the same mistake

1. **The empty-circle trap.** Placing or self-placing at 8 (or 9) and stopping there. Kakuan added 9 and 10 precisely to correct this. If Empty is current and the Marketplace is not in play at all, name it gently: the circle is not the last picture.
2. **Marketplace pride.** Placing at 10 while being ordinary still carries a secret edge of feeling above the ordinary. The pride of having come back is the last thing the tenth picture asks to put down. Noticing it is part of the work, not a mark against the person.
3. **Resistance is information.** Pushing against Searching may signal inflation (refusing to be a beginner again). Pushing against the Marketplace may signal attachment to seeker identity, or to emptiness. Pushing against Working may signal effort being mistaken as beneath one. Surface gently, never as accusation. What we refuse tends to be the next thing asking for us.
4. **Map humility.** Holding this map as the true account, rather than one useful description among several, is itself part of where someone is. Other maps describe overlapping ground in different language.

**Calibration is a service, not a judgement.** An honest Taming the Ox gives better guidance than a flattered empty circle.

## Cross-map connections

The Ox-Herding map shares ground with any contemplative-stage map: the Yin & Yang (Neidan) path describes a parallel arc of effort softening into stillness and spaciousness, and the 16 insight knowledges (ñāṇas) and the nine stages of śamatha describe overlapping ground in different language. The Hero's Journey shares the structure of departure and return with the elixir, which rhymes with the marketplace. Surface a cross-map line only as genuine synthesis when both maps show signal, never as noise.
