# 16 Ñāṇas framework — canonical content (NUM-300)

The reference the 16 Ñāṇas Map and `/sixteen-nanas-questionnaire` draw from. **Use as reference, not script.** The map output should match where the user's practice actually is, in their language. Never quote these layers verbatim.

Frame for audiences drawn to insight meditation, vipassana, noting practice, and contemplative work. Keep the Pali out of question copy; it appears on result screens and in maps as a secondary gloss, translated names primary.

**Core principle — where you are, not who you are.** This map reads a *current phase* of a cycle, not a fixed identity or a rank. A practitioner in Pre-Cycle is not "behind" one in Equanimity. Stages describe what is happening in the mind; they are not categories a person *is*, and the map is not a leaderboard.

---

## The arc

The sixteen insight knowledges (*ñāṇas*) come from the Theravada Progress of Insight (the Visuddhimagga, as taught in the Mahasi lineage), brought into modern language by contemporary insight teachers. They describe the sequence the mind tends to move through as it begins to see experience clearly: the building blocks of experience, the way they arise and pass, the difficult stretch in the middle, the rest before something shifts, and the looking-back afterwards.

**This map is a cycle, not a ladder.** The mind moves through these stages on the way to a path moment, then begins again. Practitioners go round more than once, sometimes many times, often faster and with more equanimity on later passes, sometimes with new difficult stretches. The same territory reads differently on a third pass than a first. Two non-adjacent territories can both be honestly live when the cycle is turning fast. Never imply the journey is "done" at any placement: after Reviewing (16), the cycle begins again.

The 16 stages are grouped into **6 bands** (the questionnaire's unit), with Pre-Cycle as an off-map seventh position before stage 1.

| Band | Stages | Band theme |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Cycle** | off-map | practice without a clear insight arc, or between cycles |
| **Early Insights** | 1-3 | distinguishing experience, seeing causes, three characteristics |
| **Arising & Passing** | 4 | vivid birth-and-death of phenomena |
| **Dark Night** | 5-10 | dissolution, dread, disgust, and re-observation |
| **Equanimity** | 11 | steady, impartial awareness of formations |
| **Path & Review** | 12-16 | threshold, path moment, fruition, and looking back |

Band order on the arc (used for adjacency and rapid-cycling checks): Pre-Cycle (0) · Early Insights (1) · Arising & Passing (2) · Dark Night (3) · Equanimity (4) · Path & Review (5).

The Dark Night band is also called "the dukkha ñāṇas"; Path & Review is "the threshold" in plain language. Use the plain name first.

### The 16 stages

| # | Translated name (primary) | Pali | Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distinguishing Mental & Physical | Nāmarūpa Pariccheda Ñāṇa | Early Insights | Experience is seen as just mind and matter (sensations, thoughts, intentions) rather than a solid "me" standing behind it all. |
| 2 | Cause & Effect | Paccaya Pariggaha Ñāṇa | Early Insights | Each moment conditions the next: intention before movement, contact before feeling. Experience runs on cause and effect, with no controller pulling the strings. |
| 3 | Three Characteristics | Sammasana Ñāṇa | Early Insights | Impermanence, unsatisfactoriness and not-self start to be felt, not just thought. Insight becomes experiential rather than conceptual. |
| 4 | Arising & Passing | Udayabbaya Ñāṇa | Arising & Passing | Phenomena are seen vividly arising and vanishing. Often bright, energising, even blissful. A clear, encouraging stretch. |
| 5 | Dissolution | Bhaṅga Ñāṇa | Dark Night | Attention favours the passing-away of things. Objects feel less solid; awareness rests on dissolving, ungraspable experience. |
| 6 | Fear | Bhaya Ñāṇa | Dark Night | The relentless dissolution can feel quietly frightening; nothing holds still to stand on. A sign of deepening, not of doing something wrong. |
| 7 | Disadvantages (often called Misery) | Ādinava Ñāṇa | Dark Night | Conditioned experience looks unsatisfactory, even tedious. The drawbacks of clinging to anything that won't last become plain. |
| 8 | Disenchantment (often called Disgust) | Nibbidā Ñāṇa | Dark Night | Weariness with the endless arising and passing. Practice can feel flat; the mind quietly turning away from grasping. |
| 9 | Desire for Deliverance | Muñcitukamyatā Ñāṇa | Dark Night | A strong wish to be free of it all, often to get away from the sit altogether. Restless and uncomfortable, and a real turning point. |
| 10 | Reflective Contemplation (often called Re-observation) | Paṭisaṅkhā Ñāṇa | Dark Night | Renewed effort and investigation, often through discomfort, poking at how to get free, working toward a steadier balance. |
| 11 | Equanimity | Saṅkhārupekkhā Ñāṇa | Equanimity | A spacious balance. Whatever arises or passes is met evenly, without push or pull. Often the smoothest, most restful stretch. |
| 12 | Conformity | Anuloma Ñāṇa | Path & Review | A brief moment where the mind aligns fully with the truth of things, smoothing the way for a deeper shift. |
| 13 | Change of Lineage | Gotrabhū Ñāṇa | Path & Review | The threshold moment: the mind turns away from the conditioned toward the unconditioned. |
| 14 | Path Knowledge | Magga Ñāṇa | Path & Review | A momentary direct realisation that uproots certain habits for good. |
| 15 | Fruition | Phala Ñāṇa | Path & Review | Resting in the result of that realisation; the fruit of the path, a taste of peace beyond the conditioned. |
| 16 | Reviewing | Paccavekkhaṇā Ñāṇa | Path & Review | The mind looks back at what happened: what changed, what was released, what still remains. |

Stages 12-15 pass in moments, classically within a single sitting. In lived terms a person rarely knows "I am in Conformity"; they know the threshold felt close, or that something cessation-like happened, or that they are now looking back. Read the Path & Review band through those three textures.

**Classical markers** (from the Mahasi descriptions; useful for serious practitioners, never as a checklist):

- **1** rising/falling known as matter, knowing as mind; the five senses distinguished clearly; only mind and matter, no permanent self.
- **2** matter conditioning mind and vice versa; volition preceding action.
- **3** the three characteristics; beginning, middle and end of objects; the "imperfections of insight" may start to appear.
- **4** several stages within each rise and fall; clear beginnings and endings; continuity visibly breaking.
- **5** form vanishing first, then the knowing of it; objects vague and distant.
- **6** a fear unlike ordinary fear; mind and matter seen as insubstantial; no enjoyment.
- **7** rise and fall vague and obscure; irritable, negative feelings.
- **8** boredom and sadness, "as though separated from what one loves"; an inclination to search for release.
- **9** itching, restlessness, wishing to get away and give up practice.
- **10** sensations like being pierced by splinters; stiffness; feeling as heavy as stone.
- **11** neither frightened nor glad; good concentration; time forgotten during practice.
- **12** the noble truths appear clearly. **13** between worldly and supramundane; awareness takes the unconditioned as object. **14** some fetters cut; doubt about the path ends. **15** follows immediately on 14. **16** contemplation of the path, of what was abandoned and what remains.

## Pre-Cycle (off-map)

- **Essence:** practice building foundations (concentration, ethics, everyday awareness) without a clear insight arc active right now, or ordinary life between cycles.
- **Lived markers:** the usual noise of plans and memories; general mindfulness without a map; sensations feel solid and continuous; "I'm not tracking stages, I'm showing up."
- **Gift:** ground. A practice that keeps showing up is what lets the cycle start.
- **Pitfall:** treating it as wasted time, or forcing insight before continuity is there.
- **First station ahead:** Distinguishing Mental & Physical (1), telling mental events from physical sensations as they happen.
- **App reading copy:** *"Your practice is building foundations — samatha, ethics, or everyday awareness — without a clear insight arc active right now. That is valid. The first station on the path is distinguishing mind and body in each moment."*

## Early Insights (1-3)

- **Essence:** the first clear seeing. Mind and body as distinct streams; experience as a chain of conditions; impermanence, unsatisfactoriness and not-self felt directly.
- **Lived markers:** curious, analytical; noticing categories (sensation, feeling tone, mental images); sensations starting to break into smaller units; a slightly destabilised "this isn't what I thought it was."
- **Gift:** honest seeing. Language for experience that wasn't there before.
- **Pitfall:** overclaiming early discernment, or pulling back when what seemed solid starts to come apart.

## Arising & Passing (4)

- **Essence:** the vivid seeing of birth and death of moments. Often energy, rapture, light, vibration, deep clarity; sometimes overwhelming.
- **Lived markers:** rapid flicker of micro-moments, "almost cinematic"; reality like a strobe; progress feels explosive; a wish to tell people about it.
- **Gift:** clarity and confidence in practice. Many practitioners date their real practice from here.
- **Pitfall:** mistaking the peak for the goal; chasing it afterwards. "What arrives when reached for tends to be a worse version of what arrives on its own." The brightness passes; the difficult stretch that usually follows is a stage, not a failure.

## Dark Night (5-10)

- **Essence:** the difficult middle. The mind now sees dissolution more than arising, and that seeing passes through dread, the drawbacks of everything, weariness, the urge to escape, and a heavy renewed effort.
- **Lived markers:** things dissolving before they fully form; hollow dread; beauty and meaning feeling thin; practice feeling pointless; wanting out of the sit, or out of everything; restless investigation.
- **Gift:** endurance, and a turning away from grasping that later becomes freedom.
- **Pitfall:** taking the territory as a verdict on one's life or practice; exhausting oneself; mistaking the heaviness for the end of the road.

**The Dark Night is difficult, normal and passing.** It is real territory, not a diagnosis and not a failure. Don't pathologise it, don't celebrate it: witness it. The tradition is clear on two things: it is a stage rather than a verdict on your life, and it ends. Most people on their first pass at some point decide the practice has broken them. It has not. Equanimity is closer than it feels.

**Care line (shown whenever the live terrain is the Dark Night):**
> *You place yourself in the difficult ground of the arc. Two things the tradition is clear about: it is a stage rather than a verdict on your life, and it ends. Keep your sleep, your food and your people close, and if you have access to a teacher this is the part of the arc worth telling them about. If anything here is more than difficult — if you are not safe — please talk to someone who can help today rather than to a map.*

If the user signals they are not safe, stop the questionnaire. Their safety comes before any reading.

## Equanimity (11)

- **Essence:** steady, impartial awareness of formations. Neither frightened nor glad.
- **Lived markers:** composed watching without a story; spacious patience; strong concentration with little drama; time forgotten; less reactivity, more room.
- **Gift:** balance and non-reactivity. Trusting steadiness before any leap.
- **Pitfall:** watching for "what comes next" (the one reliable way to keep it at arm's length); mistaking numbness or giving up for equanimity.

## Path & Review (12-16)

- **Essence:** the threshold, the cessation event, and the reflective aftermath.
- **Lived markers:** equanimity deepening into something close to giving way; a break in experience, a discontinuity, a quiet that wasn't there before; a new baseline; "less identified, more interested in what remains"; looking back at what changed.
- **Gift:** a shift in how one is held by experience; integration.
- **Pitfall:** building a story about practice around one event; expecting the next cycle to be easy.

**A path moment is never inferred from one experience.** Path moments are real events for some practitioners and contested constructs for others; allow both readings. A questionnaire answer, or a single vivid entry, can say the user *describes* a threshold-like shift. It never certifies a path moment. Hold any Path & Review placement as a hypothesis, look for repetition and lasting change in ordinary life, and suggest a teacher's read where it matters. A user who distrusts the "path moment" framing has not failed the map.

## States (Q2 and Q3)

Each band resolves to one state:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Active** | the terrain you are in now |
| **Familiar** | known country, can return |
| **Emerging** | gathering at the edge |
| **Dormant** | not in play |
| **Pushed against** (blocked) | actively avoided |

Typical shape: 1 Active band, 1-2 Familiar, 0-1 Emerging, 0-1 Pushed against. On a cyclical arc several bands can honestly be Active at once; nothing is demoted.

**Rapid-cycling note (when two non-adjacent bands are both Active):**
> *You marked two bands that do not sit next to each other as both active. That usually means the cycle is moving quickly rather than that the reading is wrong: on a short cycle a person can pass through several territories in a day. Treat the reading as a snapshot rather than a settled place, and take it again in a week if it matters.*

**Pushed-against note (when any band is blocked):**
> *You push against one of the bands. On this arc that is worth noting rather than fixing: the territory people most refuse is usually the one they are closest to, and the refusal is part of how it announces itself.*

What blocking tends to mean: pushing against the Dark Night means cycling without moving through; pushing against Equanimity means mistaking heat (Arising & Passing, Reflective Contemplation) for the goal; pushing against Pre-Cycle means refusing ordinary ground. Surface it gently as work asking to be done.

**Per-band reading lines** (Q2 baseline, adapt): Active → "The live terrain: [band theme]." Familiar → "Known country: [band theme]." Emerging → "Gathering at the edge: [band theme]." Pushed against → "You push against this: [band theme]."

## Crossings (thresholds between bands)

| Crossing | From band | Practice suggestion |
|---|---|---|
| From building ground into the arc beginning | Pre-Cycle | Continuity is what starts this, not effort. Sit daily and shorten the gaps between noticing rather than trying to notice harder. |
| From seeing parts into everything speeding up | Early Insights | Keep the noting steady and do not chase the vividness when it comes. What arrives when reached for tends to be a worse version of what arrives on its own. |
| From the vividness into the difficult ground after it | Arising & Passing | This is the crossing that catches people off guard, and the single most useful thing is to know it is a stage rather than a verdict on your life. Keep your sleep, your food and your people close. Consider telling a teacher where you are before you need to. |
| From the difficult ground into steadiness | Dark Night | Stop trying to get out. Equanimity on this arc arrives by staying with what is here rather than by fixing it, and the wanting-out is itself part of the territory. |
| From steadiness toward whatever comes next | Equanimity | Nothing to add and nothing to do. Watching for it is the one reliable way to keep it at arm's length. |
| From looking back into the next turn of the cycle | Path & Review | Let the review take as long as it takes, and expect the next cycle to feel like starting over. It is not: the same ground reads differently on a second pass. |

## Cycle history readings

| Cycles | Reading |
|---|---|
| This is the first time round | This is your first pass, which means everything on the arc is arriving as news. Two things are worth knowing in advance: the territory after the vividness is famously hard and famously temporary, and almost nobody gets through a first cycle without at some point deciding the practice has broken them. It has not. |
| Something like this has happened once before | A second pass tends to be recognisable rather than easier, and recognising it is worth a great deal. What surprised you last time is what to watch for now. |
| Several times | Several cycles in, the arc stops being news and starts being weather you can read. The risk shifts from being blindsided to being bored: treating known territory as already understood is how a cycle stalls. |
| Many times. I have lost count | Many cycles in, this map is describing something you already know from the inside, and it will be cruder than your own sense of it. Take the placement as a rough marker rather than a reading, and trust your own account where they disagree. |
| I genuinely do not know | Not knowing is a common and reasonable answer. The arc is much clearer in retrospect than from inside, and cycles are easier to count years later than while one is running. |

## Placement (from evidence)

- Place the user at one **live band** (and a specific stage only where the evidence names its texture), with any **familiar** bands, an **emerging** band at the edge, and any band pushed against.
- Be conservative. Single vivid entries add evidence, not a verdict. Prefer repeated texture over fresh anecdote.
- **Never place in Path & Review from one entry or one experience.** Require repeated description of a lasting shift in ordinary life, and even then hold it as hypothesis.
- **Never read the Dark Night as pathology.** If entries describe the territory, name it as a stage, and add the care line.
- Generic words ("path," "change," "reflect," "calm") appear in all kinds of entries; weight them only when they sit in a meditation-practice context.

## Cross-map connections

The 16 Ñāṇas map shares contemplative ground with the **Yin & Yang** map (stillness and concentration practice) and, through the Dark Night, sometimes with the **Wounds & Gifts** map (old material often surfaces in the difficult stretch). Surface a cross-map line only as genuine synthesis when both maps show signal, never as noise. Never read a wound as a ñāṇa or a ñāṇa as a wound.
