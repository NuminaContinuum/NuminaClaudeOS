# Guṇasthānas framework — canonical content (NUM-303)

The reference the Guṇasthānas Map and `/gunasthanas-questionnaire` draw from. **Use as reference, not script.** The map output should match where the user's conduct actually is, in their language. Never quote these layers verbatim.

Frame for audiences drawn to ethical practice, renunciation, vows, Jain thought, and contemplative discipline more broadly. Keep the Sanskrit out of question copy; it appears on result screens and in maps with its translation.

**Core principle — where you are, not who you are.** This map reads a *current position* on a ladder of conduct, not a verdict on a person. The first stage is where the path begins, not a failing grade. Someone thirty years into practice can sit rightly and deeply at stage four. Nothing here is a purity score.

**The map is hierarchical, and saying so is the kind thing.** Do not soften it into a personality quiz. The tone that works is matter-of-fact: this is where the conduct is, and here is the next real step.

---

## The ladder

The Jain tradition maps fourteen guṇasthānas ("stations of virtue"): the soul's progress from complete delusion to final liberation, measured by the thinning of deluding karma (*mohanīya*) and the four passions (anger, pride, deceit, greed). Rendered as a **staircase** ascending from the ocean of worldly existence (*saṃsāra*) to the summit of liberation (*mokṣa*), grouped into five phases.

| # | Phase | Stage | Devanagari | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | delusion | Mithyātva | मिथ्यात्व | Wrong Belief |
| 2 | delusion | Sāsvādana | सास्वादन | Taste of Right Faith |
| 3 | delusion | Mishra | मिश्र | Mixed Belief |
| 4 | faith | Avirata Samyagdṛṣṭi | अविरत सम्यग्दृष्टि | Right Faith Without Vows |
| 5 | discipline | Deśa Virata | देश विरत | Partial Vows |
| 6 | discipline | Pramatta Sarva Virata | प्रमत्त सर्व विरत | Complete Vows with Negligence |
| 7 | discipline | Apramatta Sarva Virata | अप्रमत्त सर्व विरत | Complete Vows Without Negligence |
| 8 | purification | Apūrva Karaṇa | अपूर्व करण | Unprecedented Spiritual Progress |
| 9 | purification | Anivṛtti Karaṇa | अनिवृत्ति करण | Advanced Purification |
| 10 | purification | Sūkṣma Sāmparāya | सूक्ष्म सम्पराय | Subtle Passions |
| 11 | liberation | Upaśānta Moha | उपशांत मोह | Passions Suppressed |
| 12 | liberation | Kṣīṇa Moha | क्षीण मोह | Passions Destroyed |
| 13 | liberation | Sayogi Kevalī | सयोगी केवली | Omniscient with Activity |
| 14 | liberation | Ayogi Kevalī | अयोगी केवली | Omniscient without Activity |

Phase grouping for the side-rail: **delusion → faith → discipline → purification → liberation**.

Naming on result screens and maps: the transliterated Sanskrit name is the stage name, followed by its translation and number, e.g. **Avirata Samyagdṛṣṭi** (Right Faith Without Vows, stage 4). The Devanagari is an optional gloss.

## The placeable range — the design decision that makes this map usable

The fourteen stages are not equally reportable.

| Stages | Treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1-7 | **Placeable** | The householder and early-ascetic range. Someone can honestly recognise their own conduct here. |
| 8-10 | **Visible, not claimable** | The ascent ladder (the karmic purification stages). Shown above as what lies ahead; never an answer option, never a placement. |
| 11-14 | **Narrative only** | Subsidence and destruction of deluding karma, omniscience, final liberation. No honest self-report exists. Shown as the summit, described, never offered and never placed. |

No question, probe, deep-dive or map inference ever places anyone above stage 7. If entries or answers seem to describe stages 8-14, read them as stage 7 at most, or as the ascent seen from below.

## Stage content (1-7, placeable)

Each stage: **meaning** (the map's plain-language voice, the one used for readings), **classical description**, **characteristics**, and **the next real step** (one small, concrete, keepable move; never an exhortation).

### 1. Mithyātva — Wrong Belief *(delusion)*

- **Meaning:** The starting point. Living on autopilot, identified with reactions and largely unaware of your deeper nature. Not a verdict, just where the path begins.
- **Classical:** The soul dwells in complete delusion, unable to distinguish right from wrong faith. All four passions (anger, pride, deceit, greed) operate at full intensity. The ocean of worldly existence (*saṃsāra*).
- **Characteristics:** no awareness of the soul's true nature (*jīva*); attachment and aversion govern all actions; bondage of deluding karma (*mohanīya*) at maximum.
- **Lived markers:** reactions run the day; the question has not opened yet; nothing is being held, so nothing breaks.
- **Next real step:** Notice one reaction a day, after the fact, without trying to change it yet. Naming it is the whole practice for now.

### 2. Sāsvādana — Taste of Right Faith *(delusion)*

- **Meaning:** A faint after-taste of clarity, usually as it slips away. A reminder that something truer is possible, even if you can't hold onto it yet.
- **Classical:** After briefly tasting right faith and falling back, the soul retains a residual flavour of truth. Like one who has tasted honey cannot forget its sweetness, yet the grip of delusion returns.
- **Characteristics:** unstable glimpse of spiritual truth; residual impression (*saṃskāra*) of right view persists; an impermanent state.
- **Lived markers:** something opened once, clearly, and closed; half-looking for it since; remembering the taste.
- **Next real step:** Write down what you saw when it was clear, in your own words. What is written can be returned to; what is only remembered fades.

### 3. Mishra — Mixed Belief *(delusion)*

- **Meaning:** A wavering, in-between place. Genuine insight and old confusion mixed together, neither fully winning. Honest uncertainty rather than false clarity.
- **Classical:** Right and wrong beliefs coexist, like curds mixed with treacle. The soul oscillates between clarity and confusion, unable to commit fully to either.
- **Characteristics:** alternation between right and wrong view; spiritual indecision and wavering; neither pure faith nor pure delusion.
- **Lived markers:** some weeks clear, some weeks fog; promises made and broken; appetite wins; arguing both sides.
- **Next real step:** Pick the single thing you are most divided about and stop arguing both sides for one week. Watch what that costs.

### 4. Avirata Samyagdṛṣṭi — Right Faith Without Vows *(faith)*

- **Meaning:** Right seeing dawns: you glimpse what's true and what isn't, even though your behaviour hasn't caught up yet. Vision arrives before discipline.
- **Classical:** True right faith (*samyak darśana*) is firmly attained. The soul perceives the distinction between self and non-self and the nature of karma. No vows of restraint have been undertaken; knowledge leads, conduct has not yet followed.
- **Characteristics:** firm conviction in the path (*tattvas*); clear knowledge of soul, karma and liberation; passions moderated but not restrained by vow.
- **Lived markers:** "I know what is true and I have not changed accordingly"; believing in vows without having taken one; the gap between what is said to matter and what is done.
- **Next real step:** Choose one specific thing you already know is wrong for you, name it out loud, and stop it for thirty days. One is enough.
- **Note:** This is the most common honest placement for serious, thoughtful people. Seeing arrives before behaviour does, for almost everyone.

### 5. Deśa Virata — Partial Vows *(discipline)*

- **Meaning:** You begin living by your insight in part, taking on some real commitments and restraints without overhauling your whole life at once.
- **Classical:** The householder takes the partial vows (*aṇuvratas*), the five minor vows: non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), truth (*satya*), non-stealing (*asteya*), chastity (*brahmacarya*), non-attachment (*aparigraha*). Some restraint, not complete renunciation. The layperson's path of ethical discipline within worldly life.
- **Characteristics:** five minor vows kept; gross passions restrained through disciplined conduct; spiritual progress inside ordinary life.
- **Lived markers:** a short, specific list of things given up and not resumed, which could be recited to someone; pressure and tiredness bend it at the edges; the exceptions made to one's own rules.
- **Next real step:** Take your existing restraints and find the one that has quietly loosened. Restore it before adding anything new.

### 6. Pramatta Sarva Virata — Complete Vows with Negligence *(discipline)*

- **Meaning:** Wholehearted commitment to the path, though still tripped up by lapses and inattention. The vows are real; the vigilance comes and goes.
- **Classical:** All five great vows (*mahāvratas*) of the ascetic are taken. Complete renunciation, but occasional negligence (*pramāda*), carelessness of mind, speech or body, still interrupts perfect observance.
- **Characteristics:** full vows undertaken; moments of carelessness persist; oscillation between vigilance and laxity.
- **Lived markers:** a whole life organised around practice; full vows taken in a tradition or community; what breaks it is going slack, not appetite.
- **Next real step:** Track where attention goes slack rather than where appetite pulls. The lapse to work with is inattention, not desire.

### 7. Apramatta Sarva Virata — Complete Vows Without Negligence *(discipline)*

- **Meaning:** The same full commitment, now held with steady vigilance. Negligence fades and your practice becomes continuous and awake.
- **Classical:** Complete vows observed without negligence. Vigilant, alert, unbroken practice. Standing at the gateway to the two spiritual ladders (*śreṇi*).
- **Characteristics:** unbroken observance of all vows; constant mindfulness and self-awareness; gateway to the *śreṇi*.
- **Lived markers:** the commitment holds and the attention does not lapse; steadiness without effort.
- **Pitfall:** pride. Self-satisfaction at the steadiness is the specific risk here.
- **Next real step:** Guard the steadiness without congratulating it. Self-satisfaction is the specific risk at this stage.

## Stage content (8-14, never placed)

Described so the map can show what lies above. Never offered, never claimed, never inferred.

**The ascent — purification (8-10), visible, not claimable**

- **8. Apūrva Karaṇa — Unprecedented Spiritual Progress.** A surge of unprecedented inner effort, a depth of purification never reached before. Two ladders diverge here: suppression (*upaśama śreṇi*), where passions are held down for a time, and annihilation (*kṣapaka śreṇi*), where they are destroyed for good.
- **9. Anivṛtti Karaṇa — Advanced Purification.** Approaching a threshold where the passions thin dramatically. Anger, pride, deceit and greed lose most of their hold. Only the subtlest traces of attachment remain.
- **10. Sūkṣma Sāmparāya — Subtle Passions.** Only the subtlest trace of grasping remains, a trace of subtle greed (*sūkṣma lobha*). Everything else that distorted seeing has fallen away.

**The summit — liberation (11-14), narrative only**

- **11. Upaśānta Moha — Passions Suppressed.** Delusion fully calmed but only suppressed, not uprooted. A peaceful summit one slips back from. Reached only by the suppression ladder; bypassed on the annihilation ladder.
- **12. Kṣīṇa Moha — Passions Destroyed.** All deluding karma destroyed for good. Irreversible. Reached only by the annihilation ladder.
- **13. Sayogi Kevalī — Omniscient with Activity.** Omniscient knowledge (*kevala jñāna*) while still embodied and active; the *kevalī* teaches and guides.
- **14. Ayogi Kevalī — Omniscient without Activity.** All activity of body, speech and mind ceases (*śaileśī*). The last karmas fall away and the soul rises to *Siddhaśilā*, eternal liberation (*mokṣa*).

## Bands (Q1 only)

Fourteen stages is too many for one option per question, so Q1 scores five **bands** across the placeable range and resolves within the winning band by tiebreaker.

| Band | Stages | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Unexamined | 1 | Living on reaction, before the question opens. |
| Glimpsed | 2, 3 | Tasted and lost, or clarity and confusion alternating. |
| Seeing | 4 | Right seeing settled, conduct not yet followed. |
| Partial | 5 | Real, named, kept restraints inside ordinary life. |
| Committed | 6, 7 | Complete vows, distinguished only by vigilance. |

Band labels are never shown to the user except in the two-band tie prompt.

## The attainment ceiling — conduct beats self-rating

The central reading risk on this map is overclaiming. The ladder is unusually explicit about what separates one rung from the next, which makes it easy to describe and easy to claim. So every placement passes three behavioural checks, applied in order. **Caps only ever move a placement down. Nothing can move it up.** Depth of practice never inflates a placement.

1. **Vow check.** Stage 5 or above requires an actual, named, kept restraint. Otherwise cap at **stage 4**.
   > *Your answers describe clear seeing more than kept restraint. Stage five asks for something specific you have given up and not resumed. We have placed you where the conduct is; one named, kept restraint is what moves it.*
2. **Renunciation check.** Stage 6 or above requires a formal commitment made in front of someone who would notice. Otherwise cap at **stage 5**.
   > *Complete vows are a formal undertaking, not a private intention. Nothing in your answers points to one, so we have placed you at partial restraint, which is where the conduct sits. That is not a lesser life, only a different stage.*
3. **Vigilance check.** Stage 7 requires steady, non-lapsing attention. Otherwise resolve to **stage 6**.
   > *The seventh stage differs from the sixth only in that attention stops lapsing. You reported lapses, so we have placed you at the sixth. Vigilance, not more commitment, is the work from here.*

When a cap fires, the stage that was claimed stays on the profile as **emerging**, the edge being reached for, rather than vanishing.

**Calibration is information, not a verdict.** Always say what would actually move the placement. An intention is not a vow. A restraint you could name to someone else is.

**Depth note** (whenever any cap fires on the long reading):
> *Years of practice do not move you up this ladder. The rungs are conduct, and a long, serious life can sit rightly and deeply at one place on them. Nothing here is a verdict on how much you have done.*

## The four passions (*kaṣāya*)

The ladder is the progressive thinning of four passions. Q3 asks which has the strongest grip; maps can name them when entries show them.

| Passion | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Anger** | Anger is the passion that shows first and costs least to admit. On this map it is worked with by noticing how long it lasts rather than whether it arises. |
| **Pride** | Pride is the passion that hides inside good practice. It grows rather than shrinks as conduct improves, which is why the ladder keeps checking for it above stage four. |
| **Deceit** | Deceit here is rarely lying to others. It is the small management of how things look, including to yourself, and it is what keeps a lapse from being seen. |
| **Greed** | Greed on this map is wider than money: it is the reaching that does not stop when the thing is had. It is the passion the tradition treats as last to go. |

**Pride note** (pride named at stage 4 or above):
> *You named pride as the passion with the strongest grip, and you named it while describing a practice that is going well. On this ladder that combination is the expected one rather than a contradiction: pride is the passion that grows with conduct. Noticing it is the work; being pleased at having noticed is the next thing to watch.*

## Evidence signals (for reading the current stage from entries)

| Stage | Key signals |
|---|---|
| 1 Mithyātva | Autopilot, reactions running the day, no direction, denial, confusion, no question opened |
| 2 Sāsvādana | A glimpse that faded, "used to believe", "once knew", relapse, lost faith, a fleeting taste |
| 3 Mishra | Wavering, doubt, mixed, conflicted, indecisive, "sometimes I believe", oscillating |
| 4 Avirata Samyagdṛṣṭi | Conviction, seeing clearly, right view, understanding without changed conduct, "I know but I don't" |
| 5 Deśa Virata | Named vows or restraints kept, vegetarian/vegan as an ethical vow, ahiṃsā, householder ethics, specific abstentions |
| 6 Pramatta Sarva Virata | Full renunciation or vows in a community, monastic life, lapses of attention, negligence, "I forget" |
| 7 Apramatta Sarva Virata | Unbroken vigilance, steady and constant attention, continuous awake practice |

Keyword vocabulary to weight toward the ladder: *jain / tattva / ahimsa / non-violence / anuvrata / mahavrata / vow / restraint / renunciation / fasting / tapas / austerity / vigilance / negligence / pramada / karma / passions / anger / pride / deceit / greed*.

Words that point to stages 8-14 (*kevala, moksha, shreni, omniscient, siddha, liberation*) are read as interest in the summit, never as evidence of being there.

## Placement (from evidence)

- Place the user at one **current stage** in 1-7, plus a faint **edge** on the rung just above when there is signal for it.
- Be conservative. On this ladder an honest reading rounds down, never up. When evidence is split between two stages, take the lower.
- Run the attainment ceiling on map evidence too: no named, kept restraint in the entries → not above 4; no formal commitment → not above 5; reported lapses → not 7.
- Most thoughtful readers live honestly at stages 1-4. Stage 5 needs specific, kept, nameable restraints in the entries, not values.

## Voice

- Wise, sincere, grounded. Witness before wisdom.
- Matter-of-fact, never shaming. The map is hierarchical; pretending otherwise makes it dishonest rather than kind.
- Never shame the low placements. Stage 1 is "not a verdict, just where the path begins."
- The Jain frame is ethical and specific (*ahiṃsā*, *aparigraha*). Describe the vows plainly rather than translating them into generic wellness language.
- A cap is information, not a verdict. Say what would move it.
- Sanskrit appears on result screens and maps with translations, never in answer copy.

## Cross-map connections

The Guṇasthānas map reads **conduct**; most other maps read inner states. Surface a cross-map line only as genuine synthesis when both maps show signal, never as noise. Natural pairings: the **Wounds & Gifts** map (the reactions that run stage 1 often wear a wound's shape), and the **Yin & Yang** map (a practice of stillness that has not yet become a practice of restraint, or the reverse).
