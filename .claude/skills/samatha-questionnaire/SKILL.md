# /samatha-questionnaire — The Nine Śamatha Stages questionnaire

One skill for the concentration assessment. It runs in stages and knows where the user is:

- **Q1 — Quick placement** (3-5 min). Places the user at their current stage of the nine, via the dominant band and a one-question tiebreaker. Confidence after Q1: **rough**.
- **Q2 — Full band profile** (10-12 min). Resolves a state per band, the specific stage within the live bands, and runs over-rating detection. Confidence after Q2: **deeper**.
- **Q3 — Settled reading** (20-30 min). A long reading built on what the user actually sat through, capped by their practice history. Confidence after Q3: **settled**.

The canonical content for all 9 stages (classical names, the six powers, thangka imagery, markers, gift, pitfall, the next crossing, obstacles, evidence signals, over-rating diagnostics, practice-history ceilings) lives in `samatha-framework.md`, in this skill's folder. Both this questionnaire and `/maps samatha` draw from it. Adapt the tone to where the user actually is; do not quote it verbatim.

The map itself is generated by `/maps samatha`. This skill feeds it structured readings and offers to refresh it afterward.

**Core principle — where you are, not who you are.** This map tracks one skill, sustained attention on a chosen object. A placement at the third stage is the practice working, never a lesser self. The map shows where the sits are now, not a race to the ninth.

**Placement stays conservative.** The later stages are the easiest in the system to over-claim, because they are technical, well described, and inviting. Ties resolve to the earlier band, several claimed "current" bands resolve to the earliest, and practice detail (sit length, pliancy, what actually happened in a sit) outweighs self-description.

**The 9 stages (5 bands):** Establishing (1 Placement of the Mind, 2 Continuous Placement) · Returning (3 Repeated Placement, 4 Close Placement) · Refining Quality (5 Taming, 6 Pacifying) · Subtle Work (7 Fully Pacifying, 8 Single-Pointed Attention) · Effortless (9 Equipoise). Each stage also carries its "power" label (Power of study, Power of memory, and so on); see the framework.

**Questions stay in plain practice language.** No stage numbers, band names or Sanskrit in the answer options. The classical names, powers and terms appear only on the result screen, translated names primary.

## Step 0 — Figure out where the user is

Read `context-library/maps/samatha.md` if it exists and check which stages have run (`## Q1 Reading`, `## Q2 Reading`). Route:

- **Nothing done yet** → run Q1.
- **Q1 done, Q2 not** → offer Q2: *"You've got the rough placement. Ready for a closer look? The next reading (about ten minutes) looks at every band, resolves the specific stage, and checks the stage you'd name against how you actually sit."* If they decline, stop gracefully.
- **Q1 and Q2 done, Q3 not** → offer Q3: *"You've seen the full profile. The long reading (20-30 minutes) asks you to describe a recent sitting in detail, and that description places you more reliably than any stage you could name. Want to do it now, or another time?"* If they decline, stop gracefully.
- **All three done** → offer to re-run a stage for a fresh read, or refresh the map from everything since. This map rewards re-reading: retaking Q3 every three to six months shows real movement.

Never push toward deeper stages. Q2 and Q3 are standalone-capable if the user asks for them directly. Honour an explicit stage request.

## Q1 — Quick placement

### Q1 Step 1 — Open

Present this intro, then begin immediately.

> *Where are you on the nine stages of śamatha right now?*
>
> *The classic nine stages map how concentration gathers, stabilises and equilibrates, from first placing attention on an object to effortless balance. It's a map of one skill: sustained attention.*
>
> *This short read is for honest self-assessment, not a verdict on your practice. Answer for the sitting you actually do, not the one you'd like to.*
>
> *Eight questions. Three to five minutes. Pick the closest fit.*

### Q1 Step 2 — Present the questions

One at a time. Wait for each answer. Accept the letter only. **No commentary between questions.** Option order is shuffled per question and band names are hidden, so the result can't be gamed. The order below is the app's shuffled order (a Latin square for Q1-Q5, fixed shuffles for Q6-Q8); use it or reshuffle. **The parenthetical tag is for your scoring only — never show it to the user.**

On Q7, you may add the app's hint: *"Honest averages help calibrate."*

**1. When you sit, what happens first?**
(a) I am still learning to place attention on the object and bring it back *(Establishing)*
(b) I return often — placement is getting closer but still patchy *(Returning)*
(c) Gross wandering is quieter; subtle dullness or excitement is the main work *(Refining Quality)*
(d) Sessions are stable — fine tuning and rare corrections *(Subtle Work)*
(e) Attention often rests on its own with little steering *(Effortless)*

**2. Your relationship to distraction right now is…**
(a) I notice drift quickly and re-place without much drama *(Returning)*
(b) Agitation and sinking are subtle — I am learning their texture *(Refining Quality)*
(c) Distraction is infrequent and does not derail the sit *(Subtle Work)*
(d) The monkey of distraction rarely leads the session *(Effortless)*
(e) Mind runs — I chase it with effort and instruction *(Establishing)*

**3. Pick the sentence that feels most honest:**
(a) I am taming quality — not too tight, not too dull *(Refining Quality)*
(b) Clarity and ease can coexist for long stretches *(Subtle Work)*
(c) Effort is light — calm feels like the default *(Effortless)*
(d) I am building the habit — the flame of effort burns bright *(Establishing)*
(e) Fixing is improving — I see when I slip and recover *(Returning)*

**4. What do you want from a map like this?**
(a) Orientation near single-pointed stability *(Subtle Work)*
(b) Gentle check-in on effortless placement *(Effortless)*
(c) Grounding — where does concentration training begin? *(Establishing)*
(d) Language for the returning phase without overclaiming *(Returning)*
(e) Help with subtle dullness and subtle excitement *(Refining Quality)*

**5. After a sit, you usually feel…**
(a) Naturally gathered — little sense of forcing *(Effortless)*
(b) Glad I showed up — still much re-placing ahead *(Establishing)*
(c) More collected — attention was workable most of the time *(Returning)*
(d) Cleaner — obstacles felt workable, not catastrophic *(Refining Quality)*
(e) Deeply steady — worldly concerns felt distant *(Subtle Work)*

**6. How much correction does your mind need on the object?**
(a) Rare — attention stays without much intervention *(Subtle Work)*
(b) Constant — I am re-placing almost continuously *(Establishing)*
(c) Almost none — equipoise carries the session *(Effortless)*
(d) Frequent at first, then stretches of holding *(Returning)*
(e) Periodic — mostly fine-tuning laxity or excitement *(Refining Quality)*

**7. Typical sit length when you practice (honest average):**
(a) About 15–25 minutes — workable but often interrupted *(Returning)*
(b) Under 15 minutes — still building the container *(Establishing)*
(c) About 25–40 minutes — enough time to work with subtlety *(Refining Quality)*
(d) Often over an hour — concentration sustains itself *(Effortless)*
(e) About 40–60 minutes — long stable stretches *(Subtle Work)*

**8. If you had to name your dominant climate this month…**
(a) Refining — subtle dullness and excitement in focus *(Refining Quality)*
(b) Beginning — placing and lengthening are the work *(Establishing)*
(c) Subtle — pacification and one-pointed stretches *(Subtle Work)*
(d) Returning — close placement, confidence growing *(Returning)*
(e) Effortless — balanced placement, little heroics *(Effortless)*

### Q1 Step 3 — Score the band

Tally **band content tags** (not letter positions) across Q1-Q8. The band with the most answers is the user's band.

**Tie handling (conservative, as shipped):**
- Take the **two earliest tied bands in path order** (Establishing → Returning → Refining Quality → Subtle Work → Effortless). A three-way tie keeps only the two earliest.
- If both tied bands have a tiebreaker → run the **combined tiebreaker** (Step 4).
- If one of them is Effortless (no tiebreaker) → drop Effortless and run the other band's tiebreaker. Effortless never wins a tie.

### Q1 Step 4 — Tiebreaker (resolve the stage)

Fire the band's tiebreaker. Effortless resolves directly to **Stage 9 — Equipoise** (no tiebreaker). Options are shuffled; tags are builder-only.

**Establishing** — *Within establishing, which fits closer?*
(a) Still learning the method — effort and instruction carry me *(Stage 1 — Placement of the Mind)*
(b) Periods on the object are lengthening — progress is visible *(Stage 2 — Continuous Placement)*

**Returning** — *Within returning, which fits closer?*
(a) Wandering eases — I catch the mind and re-place *(Stage 3 — Repeated Placement)*
(b) Recollection stays close — recovery is faster *(Stage 4 — Close Placement)*

**Refining Quality** — *Within refining quality, which fits closer?*
(a) I lead the mind — agitation no longer dictates the sit *(Stage 5 — Taming)*
(b) Gross sinking and excitement are mostly pacified *(Stage 6 — Pacifying)*

**Subtle Work** — *Within subtle work, which fits closer?*
(a) Long stable sits — subtle traces still flicker *(Stage 7 — Fully Pacifying)*
(b) Attention rests with minimal correction *(Stage 8 — Single-Pointed Attention)*

**Combined tiebreaker (two bands tied):** ask, as shipped, *"[Band A] and [Band B] tied. Which nuance fits better?"* (e.g. *"Returning and Refining Quality tied. Which nuance fits better?"*). This is the one place band names reach the user, after all eight answers are in. Present all four options from both bands, shuffled together. The pick resolves both band and stage.

### Q1 Step 5 — Calibration check (no auto-downgrade)

Compare the placed band with the band of the Q7 (sit length) answer:

- **Placed in Subtle Work or Effortless, but Q7 was under 25 minutes** (the Establishing or Returning answer) → add:
  > *Your answers lean toward later stages, but your typical sits are still relatively short. Concentration self-rating often runs ahead of clock time — that is common, not a failure. A longer read (Q2) will help calibrate.*
- **Placed in Establishing, but Q7 was 40 minutes or more** (the Subtle Work or Effortless answer) → add:
  > *You placed yourself early on the path while reporting long, stable sits. If that feels accurate, wonderful — if not, treat this as a rough sketch and refine in practice.*

### Q1 Step 6 — Render the result

**ASCII ascent first**, the nine bends bottom to top, current stage lit, a soft halo (◌) on the other stage in the same band. The elephant whitens up the path (● black → ○ white), the flame of effort (▲) burns lower. Adapt to the actual result:

```
   9  Equipoise                     ○        rainbow
   8  Single-Pointed Attention      ○  ▴
   7  Fully Pacifying               ◔  ▴
   6  Pacifying                     ◔  ▲     ← you
   5  Taming                        ◑  ▲     ◌  (same band)
   4  Close Placement               ◑  ▲
   3  Repeated Placement            ◕  ▲
   2  Continuous Placement          ●  ▲
   1  Placement of the Mind         ●  ▲

   ← you  current    ◌  same band    ● → ○  the elephant whitening    ▲  effort
```

(Mark the halo only on the same-band partner; the example above shows Stage 6 with Stage 5 haloed. Stage 9 has no partner.)

Then: *"Your placement · rough read"*, the stage number and classical name, with the power and Sanskrit as a secondary gloss (e.g. **Stage 3 · Repeated Placement** — *Power of memory, fixing · smṛti*), and a 2-3 sentence reading grounded in the user's actual answers: the lived experience, the gift, the typical pitfall. Draw on `samatha-framework.md` but adapt to what they said; do not restate generic content. Add the calibration note from Step 5 if it fired.

Example microcopy (Stage 3):
> *You're at **Repeated Placement** (Power of memory, fixing). The mind still leaves the object entirely, but you catch it faster than you used to. The gift here is the returning itself: it's not a failure, it's the practice. The pitfall is discouragement at how often you have to come back. Returning is how attention is built.*

Example microcopy (Stage 9):
> *You're at **Equipoise**, effortless concentration. The pitfall, traditional teachers warn, is mistaking momentary calm for the ninth stage. Authentic equipoise includes pliancy (praśrabdhi), a lightness and suppleness of body and mind, and stable access to deep concentration. If part of you suspects you've over-rated, trust that suspicion.*

End with the confidence label and a CTA:

> *This is a rough placement, a first location, not a verdict. Q1 places your current stage only. The 10-minute reading (Q2) shows which bands are familiar, which are emerging, and whether the stage you'd name matches how you actually sit. Confidence: rough.*

## Q2 — Full band profile + over-rating detection (10-12 min)

**Gate:** standalone-capable. If `samatha.md` has a `## Q1 Reading`, open with: *"Your first reading placed you at [Stage N — name]. This one looks at every band, resolves the specific stage, and checks the claim against how you actually sit."* Otherwise open: *"A deeper read of where you are in concentration practice. Ten to twelve minutes. We'll look at each band of the path, resolve the specific stage where your practice actually lives, and check the stage you'd name against how you actually sit."*

Q2 resolves a **state per band** — Current (the stage you are at now) / Familiar (visited, can return) / Emerging (the next stage gathering) / Dormant (not in play) / Not-yet-attempted (not reached in this practice arc). 12-15 questions: 5 scan, up to 3 stage resolutions, 4 probes, 3 Likert, in that order. One at a time, no commentary between. **State, band and stage tags are for your scoring only — never show them.** Shuffle option order (the scan lists below are already in the app's shuffled order). Scan hint: *"Answer for the sitting you actually do."*

### Q2 Section 1 — Band scan (5 questions, one per band)

**1. First putting attention on the object, brief moments of placement:** Currently where I am. The basic work of starting again and again *(Current)* / Familiar. I've been here. I can drop back into beginner's territory *(Familiar)* / Pulling at me. I sense the practice asking to be restarted from the beginning *(Emerging)* / Not in play *(Dormant)* / Not yet attempted. I haven't formally tried to establish a practice *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Establishing]*
**2. Losing the object and finding it again, growing continuity:** Familiar. The returning work is something I've done a lot of *(Familiar)* / Pulling at me. I sense I need to focus on the returning specifically *(Emerging)* / Currently where I am. The returning is the daily work *(Current)* / Not in play *(Dormant)* / Not yet attempted *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Returning]*
**3. Working with subtle dullness or subtle excitation:** Pulling at me. Subtler obstacles are starting to appear *(Emerging)* / Currently where I am. The work is the quality of attention *(Current)* / Familiar. I've worked here. I can return to this terrain *(Familiar)* / Not in play *(Dormant)* / Not yet attempted *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Refining Quality]*
**4. Attention essentially stable, refining the very subtle texture:** Familiar. I've touched this. Briefly *(Familiar)* / Pulling at me. Stability is starting to be sustainable *(Emerging)* / Currently where I am. The work is the very subtle texture of attention *(Current)* / Not in play yet *(Dormant)* / Not yet attempted *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Subtle Work]*
**5. Effortless attention, with the factors of calm abiding developing:** Not yet attempted *(Not-yet-attempted)* / Not in play. I'm honest that I haven't reached this *(Dormant)* / Familiar. I've had brief access. Whether stable, I'm not sure *(Familiar)* / Pulling at me. The frontier is the dropping of effort itself *(Emerging)* / Currently where I am. Effortless attention with the factors developing *(Current)* — *[Effortless]*

### Q2 Section 2 — Stage resolution (0-3 questions, adaptive)

Ask one question for each band the scan marked **Current or Emerging**, in path order, up to three. Effortless never needs one (it holds only Stage 9). Options shuffled, tags hidden.

**Establishing** — *Within the establishing, which is closer?* Brief continuity. I can hold the object for about a minute at most before wandering *(Stage 2)* / Almost no continuity. I'm just starting, attention barely lands before it leaves *(Stage 1)*
**Returning** — *Within the returning, which is closer?* I no longer lose the object entirely. It stays in awareness even when attention dips *(Stage 4)* / I still lose the object completely, but I catch it back quickly *(Stage 3)*
**Refining Quality** — *Within the refining, which is closer?* Subtle excitation. Attention scatters, jumps, gets slightly agitated. I'm settling it *(Stage 6)* / Subtle dullness. Attention sinks, dims, goes vague. I'm raising it *(Stage 5)*
**Subtle Work** — *Within the subtle work, which is closer?* Single-pointed once entered. Effort is needed at the start, but attention is unmoving once stable *(Stage 8)* / Subtle distractions essentially gone but effort still active throughout the sit *(Stage 7)*

### Q2 Section 3 — Context probes (4 questions)

Each answer nudges one band (+1). Shuffle order.

**6. The recent texture of your sittings, averaged over the last few weeks:** Mostly forgetting the object, trying to start again *(Establishing)* / Constant returning, but it's working *(Returning)* / Mostly stable with subtle quality issues *(Refining Quality)* / Stable with effort, refining the very subtle *(Subtle Work)* / Effortless throughout, no need to maintain *(Effortless)*
**7. The balance between your two supports:** Mindfulness is strong; I can stay with the object. Noticing what attention is doing is weak *(Establishing)* / Noticing when attention drifts is strong; staying with the object is still gappy *(Refining Quality)* / Both are well-developed and work together. I rarely lose either *(Subtle Work)* / Both are still being built. I'm new to noticing what attention does *(Establishing)* / Both have become transparent. Attention holds itself; there's no need to actively support it *(Effortless)*
**8. The length of sit you can sustain with consistent quality:** 5 to 15 minutes *(Establishing)* / 20 to 30 minutes *(Returning)* / 30 to 45 minutes *(Refining Quality)* / 45 to 60 minutes *(Subtle Work)* / 60 minutes or more, effortlessly *(Effortless)* / I am not currently sitting daily *(no nudge)* — **sit-length calibration**
**9. The bodily and mental quality of your sittings:** The body is usually a problem: restlessness, aches, the body wants to move *(Establishing)* / The body settles okay. Mind is the work. Body isn't actively a distraction *(Returning)* / Body and mind are both settled. There's an ease in the body that supports attention *(Subtle Work)* / Body and mind feel light, supple, agile. The body itself feels infused with the attention *(Effortless — pliancy)* / I am not sure what this refers to, or whether I have it *(no nudge)* — **pliancy check**

### Q2 Section 4 — Cross-cutting Likert (3 items, 1-5)

**10.** *I sometimes suspect I've rated my stage higher than my actual practice supports.* (4-5 → flag; honoured as wisdom, not flaw)
**11.** *Continuous attention on the object, and knowing what attention is doing, are both reliable in my practice.* (1-2 → flag at Stage 5+)
**12.** *My practice is daily, sustained, and has been for at least several months.* (1-2 → flag at Stage 4+)

### Q2 Step 5 — Resolve

1. **Current band.** If exactly one band is Current, use it. If none: pick from the Familiar bands (or all bands if none are Familiar) the one with the most probe nudges, earliest band on a tie, and say the placement was inferred. If several: take the **earliest** and mark the later ones Familiar (the ladder is strict).
2. **Claimed stage.** The stage resolved in Section 2 for the Current band; if none was asked (e.g. Current was inferred), the **earliest stage of that band**; Effortless → Stage 9.
3. **Over-rating flags** against the claimed stage (thresholds in `samatha-framework.md`):
   - `sit_too_short` — Stage 9 claimed and sit under an hour-plus; or Stage 7-8 claimed and sit under 45 minutes (5-15, 20-30, or not sitting daily)
   - `no_pliancy_at_stage_9` — Stage 9 claimed and Q9 was anything other than the pliancy answer
   - `self_reported_over_rating` — Q10 is 4 or 5
   - `supports_not_reliable` — Stage 5+ claimed and Q11 is 1 or 2
   - `practice_not_sustained` — Stage 4+ claimed and Q12 is 1 or 2
4. **Downgrade.** 2 flags → drop the placement **1 stage**; 3 or more → drop **2 stages** (never below Stage 1). Fewer than 2 → no change.
5. **Confidence per band.** HIGH when at least one probe nudged that band (the scan is corroborated); MEDIUM when the scan stands alone. A probe that disagrees never overturns the scan: the user's direct answer outranks an indirect signal, it just loses certainty.

### Q2 Step 6 — Render

- The nine-stage ascent (same shape as Q1), each stage styled by its band's state: ● Current (with the resolved stage marked "← you") / ◌ Familiar / ◦ Emerging at the edge / · Dormant / blank for Not-yet-attempted.
- A one-line reading per band in play, from the band theme: Current → *"Where you are now: [theme]."* Familiar → *"Known ground: [theme]."* Emerging → *"Gathering: [theme]."* Not-yet-attempted → *"Not reached in this practice arc."* Dormant → *"Not in play right now."* Expand the Current and Familiar ones to 1-2 sentences in the user's terms (classical name primary, power and Sanskrit secondary).
- **The summary**, built as shipped:
  > *You are at stage [N], [stage name]. [Familiar bands] is/are familiar ground. [Emerging bands] is gathering.* [If downgraded:] *You placed yourself at [claimed stage]; what you described about sitting supports an earlier stage, and we have read it that way.* *The ninth stage is rare. Most practitioners live for years around the second to fourth, and that is the practice working rather than failing.*
- **Calibration notes** — only when a downgrade happened, one line per flag:
  - sit_too_short: *The stage you named needs sits considerably longer than the ones you described. The phenomenology of the later stages does not appear in short sittings.*
  - no_pliancy_at_stage_9: *The ninth stage is defined by pliancy, a lightness and suppleness of body and mind, and you did not describe it. Without pliancy the attainment is not what the tradition means by it.*
  - self_reported_over_rating: *You said you suspect you have rated yourself above what your practice supports. That suspicion is usually worth trusting.*
  - supports_not_reliable: *Both supports, staying with the object and knowing what attention is doing, need to be reliable before the middle stages. You reported that they are not yet.*
  - practice_not_sustained: *The middle stages generally require sustained daily practice over months, and yours is not yet that.*
- Frame any downgrade as a more accurate map, never a demotion. Reaching Stage 4 is a substantial accomplishment. Honour a high Q10: that self-honesty is itself a sign of practice maturity.
- Confidence: **deeper**. Restate "where you are, not who you are." CTA: *"Continue to the long reading when you're ready."*

### Q2 Step 7 — Write the Q2 reading and offer refresh

Prepend a `## Q2 Reading — YYYY-MM-DD` section (above the Q1 reading) to `samatha.md` with: **Confidence: deeper**, the state per band (with confidence), the claimed and final stage, any flags and the downgrade, and the summary line. Then offer to refresh the Śamatha map (merging Q2 with Q1 + harvested notes; raises the confidence header).

## Q3 — Long reading (20-30 min)

**Gate:** aware of Q1/Q2, standalone-capable otherwise (Section 2 is the baseline). Open with the shipped note: if a prior reading exists, *"Your earlier reading placed you at [stage]. This is the long one: several questions are yours to answer in your own words, and you can stop and come back. One of them asks you to describe a recent sitting in detail, and it is the one that matters most."* Otherwise: *"This is the long reading. Several questions are yours to answer in your own words, and you can stop and come back at any point. One of them asks you to describe a recent sitting in detail, and it is the one that matters most."*

24 questions across eight sections: 9 open-ended, 12 multiple-choice, 3 Likert. One at a time, no commentary between. **Tags are for your scoring only — never show them.** Shuffle option order. Voice input welcome on the open-ended questions; the user can pause and resume.

### Q3 Section 1 — Settling in (2 open-ended)

**1.** *Tell me about your practice as it actually is, not as you would like it to be. When you sit, what happens?* (3-4 min)
**2.** *What made you sit down with this questionnaire today? Something in the practice, or something outside it?* (2 min)

### Q3 Section 2 — Band scan (5 multiple-choice)

Same five bands and state mapping as Q2, new wording. Doubles as the baseline and the selector for Section 4.

**3. Getting attention onto the object at all, and keeping it there for a breath or two:** That is the work of my sits right now *(Current)* / I know it well. I am past it, mostly *(Familiar)* / I am rebuilding it after a long gap *(Emerging)* / Not where my attention goes any more *(Dormant)* / I have not really started sitting *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Establishing]*
**4. Losing the object and bringing it back, again and again, and it working:** That is my sit. Constant return, and it holds *(Current)* / Familiar ground. It happens less now *(Familiar)* / The returning is starting to work rather than exhaust me *(Emerging)* / Not the texture of my sits *(Dormant)* / I do not get far enough for this *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Returning]*
**5. The object stays, and the work is the quality of attention — dimness, or a fine agitation:** That is where my sits live now *(Current)* / I have worked there. It comes and goes *(Familiar)* / I am beginning to notice quality rather than presence *(Emerging)* / Not yet what I am working with *(Dormant)* / Well beyond where I sit *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Refining Quality]*
**6. Gross distraction essentially gone, and effort still needed throughout:** That is my sit. Stable, and it costs effort *(Current)* / I have had stretches like that *(Familiar)* / Stability is starting to hold for longer *(Emerging)* / Not in play *(Dormant)* / Well beyond where I sit *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Subtle Work]*
**7. Attention holding itself, with no need to maintain it, for the length of a sit:** That is my sit *(Current)* / It has happened. I cannot produce it at will *(Familiar)* / Something like it is beginning to appear *(Emerging)* / Not in play *(Dormant)* / Not reached in this practice arc *(Not-yet-attempted)* — *[Effortless]*

### Q3 Section 3 — The recent sit and its obstacles (2 open-ended + 2 MC)

**8.** *Describe one recent sitting in as much detail as you can. What was the object. What happened in the first ten minutes, in the middle, near the end. Where attention went when it left. What the body was doing. How long you sat.* (5-6 min) — add the hint: *"This is the question that places you. Write it as it was, including the parts that went badly."*
**9.** *Which obstacles are most alive right now — dullness, agitation, the body, time, doubt, something else? Describe the texture of the main one. Not that it happens: what it is actually like.* (2-3 min)
**10. Averaged across the last few weeks, the texture of your sittings:** Mostly forgetting the object and starting again *(Establishing)* / Constant returning, and it is working *(Returning)* / Mostly stable, with subtle quality issues *(Refining Quality)* / Stable with effort, refining the very subtle *(Subtle Work)* / Effortless throughout, with nothing to maintain *(Effortless)*
**11. Which of these is most alive in your sitting right now?** Attention stays put and the whole thing goes dim *(Dullness → Refining Quality)* / A fine restlessness underneath, even when it is stable *(Agitation → Refining Quality)* / Aches, restlessness, the body wanting to move *(Body → Establishing)* / Finding the time at all *(Time → Establishing)* / Wondering whether any of this is going anywhere *(Doubt → Returning)*

> *Scoring:* Q8 is the single most diagnostic question in the whole ladder. Read it for sit length, how fast attention leaves and returns, whether the object is lost entirely or only dims, the texture of the obstacles, and what the body does. Q9 cross-checks it.

### Q3 Section 4 — Adaptive deep-dive (3 open-ended)

Select 3 prompts, in this priority: **1 on the Current band** (from Section 2), **1 on the band of the obstacle** picked in Q11, **1 on the Emerging band** (or the first Familiar band). Skip duplicates and fill from path order (Establishing first).

**Over-rating swap:** if over-rating is suspected, the second slot becomes the self-inquiry prompt. Suspected = Q18 (self-honesty) is 4-5, **or** the claimed stage (the earliest stage of the Current band; Effortless → 9) is above the practice-history ceiling. In the shipped order those answers come later (Q18, Q22-24), so on a first pass this usually cannot fire yet. Use it when a prior Q2 reading carries a self-reported over-rating flag, or when the user is re-taking Q3 and their earlier answers already show it.

- **Establishing** — *"Describe what happens in the first two minutes of a sit. Where does attention actually go when it leaves the object, and what brings it back? What does the body do while this is happening?"*
- **Returning** — *"Describe the returning itself. How do you notice you have gone? Is the noticing sharp or slow, and does the coming back cost effort or happen on its own? What is different about a sit where it works?"*
- **Refining Quality** — *"Describe the quality of attention when the object is no longer being lost. Where does it dim, where does it fizz? What do you do about each, and how do you tell the difference between a settled sit and a dull one?"*
- **Subtle Work** — *"Describe the effort that is still needed. What exactly are you doing to hold the sit together, and what happens in the moments you stop doing it? Where does the very subtle distraction still get in?"*
- **Effortless** — *"Describe a sit that held itself. How did it begin, and at what point did the maintaining stop being necessary? What was the body like? Could you enter it again deliberately, or did it arrive?"*
- **Over-rating self-inquiry** — *"Set the stage numbers aside for a moment. If a teacher who knew your practice well sat with you for a week, what would they say about where you actually are? Not to catch you out — what would they see that you might not?"* — with the hint: *"This one is asked because something in your answers pointed at it. There is no wrong answer, and nothing here is a test."*

**12, 13, 14.** The three selected prompts. Each 2-4 min, voice or text.

### Q3 Section 5 — Sit length, pliancy, self-reading (2 MC + 3 Likert)

**15. The length of sit you can sustain with consistent quality:** 5 to 15 minutes *(Establishing)* / 20 to 30 minutes *(Returning)* / 30 to 45 minutes *(Refining Quality)* / 45 to 60 minutes *(Subtle Work)* / 60 minutes or more, effortlessly *(Effortless)* / I am not currently sitting daily *(no nudge)*
**16. The bodily and mental quality of your sittings:** The body is usually a problem: restlessness, aches, wanting to move *(Establishing)* / The body settles well enough. Mind is the work *(Returning)* / Body and mind both settled, with an ease that supports attention *(Subtle Work)* / Body and mind light, supple, agile — the body itself infused with the attention *(Effortless — pliancy)* / I am not sure what this refers to, or whether I have it *(no nudge)*

### Q3 Section 8 — Calibration context (3 multiple-choice)

**22. How long have you practised concentration specifically — not mindfulness or meditation in general?** Less than six months / Six months to two years / Two to five years / Five to ten years / More than ten years
**23. And at what intensity, over most of that time?** Sporadically. Weeks on, months off / A few times a week / Daily, twenty minutes or so / Daily, an hour or more / Daily, with regular intensive retreats
**24. And with what guidance?** None. Entirely on my own / Books, apps and recordings / A teacher I see occasionally / A teacher who knows my practice well / I teach or guide others in this

> *Scoring:* each answer sets a ceiling (table in `samatha-framework.md`); the lowest of the three is the **maximum-plausible stage**. Depth never inflates. It only caps over-claims.

### Q3 Step 9 — Resolve

1. **Current band** from Section 2, same rules as Q2: one Current → use it; none → infer from Familiar (or all bands) by the probe nudges (Q10, Q11, Q15, Q16), with the open-ended answers breaking a tie; several → the earliest, the rest Familiar.
2. **Claimed stage** = the earliest stage of the Current band (Effortless → Stage 9). Q3 has no within-band resolution; let the open-ended answers say in prose whether the user is toward the later stage of the band, without moving the number.
3. **Flags** — the Q2 rules, using Q15 for sit length, Q16 for pliancy and Q18 for self-honesty. (Q3 does not ask the supports or consistency items, so those two flags don't apply.)
4. **Ceiling.** Over-by = claimed stage − maximum-plausible stage.
   - Over-by **2 or more** → place at the maximum-plausible stage.
   - Otherwise, **2+ flags** → drop 1 stage (3+ flags → 2), never below Stage 1.
   - Over-by exactly **1** and no downgrade → keep the placement, mark it **approaching**.
5. **Pliancy-access reading** when Stage 9 was claimed and Q16 was not the pliancy answer.
6. **Weighting.** The structured answers set the placement; the open-ended answers (above all Q8) carry the texture, the quotes and the confidence, and break ties. They never override a direct answer. If Q8 plainly contradicts the claim, say so in the reading and lean on the calibration notes rather than silently moving the stage.

### Q3 Step 10 — Render

- **"What your practice said"** — the Q21 sentence as a direct quote at the top (trim to one or two sentences if long).
- The nine-stage ascent styled by state, as in Q2, with the final stage marked "← you" and "approaching" shown if it applies.
- **The recent sit, reflected back** — 2-3 lines from Q8 in the user's words, with the markers you read in it (sit length, returning rhythm, whether the object was lost or dimmed, obstacle texture, body).
- **The summary**, built as shipped:
  > *You are at stage [N], [stage name].* [If approaching:] *Read it as approaching rather than settled.* *[Familiar bands] is/are familiar ground. [Emerging bands] is gathering.* [If downgraded:] *You placed yourself at [claimed stage]; what you described about sitting supports an earlier stage, and we have read it that way.* *The ninth stage is rare. Most practitioners live for years around the second to fourth, and that is the practice working rather than failing.*
- A 2-4 sentence reading per active band woven with the user's phrases (classical name primary, power and Sanskrit secondary).
- **The obstacle** — the framework's reading for the Q11 obstacle, tied to what they wrote in Q9.
- **The transition you're at** — the current stage's "next crossing" from the framework, made concrete with what they described. If a band is Emerging, name it as the gate gathering.
- **Notes**, each only if it applies:
  - if downgraded: the Q2 flag lines for each flag that fired, plus, if the ceiling drove it: *Your practice history sets a ceiling on what the later stages can plausibly mean yet. That is arithmetic rather than a judgement: the stages above take the years they take, and nothing you described is being doubted.*
  - if approaching: *You are a stage above what your practice history would usually support. We have left the placement where you put it and marked it as approaching rather than settled — that is the honest reading of one stage of stretch.*
  - pliancy access: *You named the ninth stage without describing pliancy, the lightness and suppleness the tradition uses to define it. The useful reading is a strong eighth stage with access to the ninth, which is a real and uncommon place to be.*
  - sporadic intensity (Q23): *Your practice is sporadic by your own account. That is worth saying plainly, because on this ladder consistency does more than technique: a short daily sit outruns a long occasional one, every time.*
  - teaches (Q24): *You teach or guide others in this. That usually sharpens a self-reading rather than clouding it, and it also means you already know how much of the map is description and how much is instruction.*
- At Stage 4 or above, one line that concentration and insight are complementary paths, if the user's profile shows interest in insight practice.
- Confidence: **settled**. Always restate "where you are, not who you are."

## Tone reminders

- Wise, sincere, grounded. Witness before wisdom. This map is technical and demanding: don't soften it into vague calmness or generic mindfulness. The stages have specific markers (Stage 4 is not losing the object; Stage 9 includes pliancy).
- No em dashes in your own reading prose. No "unlock," "transform," "breakthrough," "enlightenment." (Option copy is verbatim from the app and keeps its dashes.)
- Numina lexicon overlaps with śamatha: attention, awareness, object, breath, settling, returning, sinking, scattering, effort, ease, stability, pliancy.
- **Practice detail outweighs self-description.** The recent sit, the sit length and the body tell you more than any stage the user names.
- **Calibration is a service, not a judgement.** Most practitioners live for years at Stages 2-4. Frame any downgrade as honesty, never failure, and honour the user who suspects they over-rated.
- **"Where you are, not who you are."** The map tracks one skill. An early stage is the practice working, never a lesser self.
- Cross-reference: the Yin & Yang map's first two stages rest on a settled mind, and the Chakra map shares the body as the site of pliancy. Mention only when both maps show signal.
